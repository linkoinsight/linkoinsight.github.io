Svenska

Finance first. Then sales, purchases and inventory.

None of the packages is built yet. Here is the plan, and what each package will be checked against.

Planned

Every package has three parts

The model

A semantic model with measures and Swedish names. It matters as much as the reports: it is what makes everyone calculate alike, and it will be prepared so that your own AI answers correctly too.

The reports

Reports in Power BI that start with a question and answer it in a sentence before the chart.

The receipt

A receipt of its own after every refresh, where Business Central has a figure to check against. A package without such a figure will say so rather than pretend.

The packages

Fabricado's planned packages, what they should answer and what they are checked against
PackageWhat it should answerWhat it is checked against
Finance, first Planned Income statement, balance sheet and a monthly overview. How did the month go, and why? Business Central's trial balance, per account and month. That check already exists on the demo company.
Sales Planned Which customers are buying less than usual? Which items are selling worse than last year? The customer ledger.
Purchases Planned What do we buy, from whom, and what falls due soon? The vendor ledger.
Inventory Planned How much money is tied up in stock, and how fast does it turn over? The inventory accounts in the general ledger.

Only the check against the trial balance exists today, and only on the demo company. The checks against the customer and vendor ledgers and the inventory accounts are our plan. They are neither built nor tested.

The packages are our own models and reports in Fabric, not an add-on to Microsoft's nine free apps for Business Central. We compare our finance model with Microsoft's finance app to show what the check, Bryggan, adds. The answer key is Business Central's trial balance.

Models that are ready for AI

Chatting with your data keeps getting cheaper. What doesn't come free is a model that gives the right answers, and proof that the answer is right.

So every model will be prepared for AI: clear names, instructions and answers checked in advance. Your own Copilot or Claude reads the model under your own sign-in, through Microsoft's connection. Fabricado has no chatbot of its own.

Swedish isn't solved. According to Microsoft, Copilot in Power BI is officially supported in English only, and we haven't tested either Copilot or Claude in Swedish. We promise no answers in Swedish until the questions have been measured.

Microsoft's own advice to anyone using Copilot in Business Central is to verify important information against the underlying records. That is what Bryggan's receipt is for: an AI that quotes a figure should be able to show that the figure matches the general ledger.

Ask for a change in plain words

Planned

No standard package fits every industry. So you should be able to ask for what's missing, without a consultant. The agents work in the background; you don't chat with them. This is how it will work, with stock turnover as the example:

  1. You write what you want

    "I want to see stock turnover."

  2. An AI proposes the change

    It sees how the model is built, but no rows from your tables.

  3. Ordinary code checks the proposal

    Nothing published may be renamed or removed, the formula must parse, and the cost is estimated.

  4. You see the change in Swedish and approve it

    A named person at your company sees before and after, and can ask a follow-up question or request a small edit first. Neither the agent nor Fabricado can approve.

  5. The change is made and checked

    A copy is saved first. Then the change is made from your browser, and Bryggan checks that the figures still match.

  6. You get a receipt

    The receipt says what changed and who approved it. You can roll back with one click, and the latest copies stay in your own environment.

Where the AI will run for customers isn't decided yet, and neither is what a change will cost. AI and data on the security page.
