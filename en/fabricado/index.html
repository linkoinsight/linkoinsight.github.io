Get in touch How it will work

For those who don't know where to start

You run Business Central and want better reports. You have heard of Microsoft Fabric, but you don't know what to do, what it costs or how it's done. You have no BI team of your own.

Fabricado is being built for exactly that. We will take you all the way: show what is needed and what it costs before you decide, install everything in your own Microsoft environment, check the figures against the general ledger and keep watch every day. With clear views of the data, and an alarm when something has stopped.

What it will cost, with Microsoft's share

We plan SEK 2,500 a month per Business Central company, however many people read the reports, and a setup fee of SEK 9,500, which counts toward the first months if you continue. Excluding VAT.

Microsoft charges for the rest: a Fabric capacity and a Power BI Pro licence per reader. With ten readers, Microsoft's share is SEK 2,983 a month with a one-year reservation of the capacity, or SEK 4,103 without one. With the capacity and Pro, that is SEK 5,483–6,603 a month. If the readers have no Business Central licence, add SEK 765. These are Microsoft's list prices on 1 November 2026, excluding VAT.

What exists, and what is planned

Every part carries one of four labels. We would rather be too strict than too kind.

  • Available

    Exists today and can be read or seen here, without an account.

  • Proven on demo data

    Run on Microsoft's demo company, and the receipt can be read here. You can't run it yourselves yet.

  • Depends on a Microsoft preview

    Depends on something Microsoft hasn't released yet.

  • Planned

    Not built, or built but not online. It can't be used yet.

Knowing what has to be done

  • Help pages and answers

    Available

    The steps at Microsoft, who does what, known errors and answers to the most common questions, in Swedish and English. They are on this site.

  • A free check before you buy

    Planned

    It will read your Microsoft environment and tell you what is missing and what it costs, before you decide. The part that reads the account, the approval, the capacity, the settings and the access to Business Central is built into the app, but the app isn't online. It reads licences only for the person signed in. The price and the Business Central version aren't built.

  • The app and the guided start

    Planned

    The app exists and runs with us on demo data, but it isn't online and no outsider has tried it. It is in Swedish only so far. The goal is plug and play: you click and read, nobody writes code. Today it takes about ten steps across three roles.

Knowing what it costs

  • The cost table

    Available

    Our planned fee next to the customer's Microsoft bill, dated, with the arithmetic in plain view.

  • The cost page in the app

    Planned

    The capacity, the Pro licences, whether overage is on, the storage and how much of the capacity the jobs use, against a limit you set yourselves. The limit warns but stops nothing.

  • Cost efficiency

    Planned

    When Microsoft releases something that could make your setup cheaper, we measure before and after on your own capacity and show the result. We promise no saving in advance: a faster job only lowers the invoice if the capacity can shrink or pause.

Getting the data in and checking it

  • Bryggan: the check against the general ledger

    Proven on demo data

    Proven on Microsoft's demo company, never on a customer's figures. You can read the receipt here as a file, but you can't run Bryggan yourselves yet.

  • The install into your Fabric

    Planned

    Built. From the browser it has created all seven items in our trial, but it isn't fully tested and it isn't online.

  • The fetch from Business Central

    Planned

    Built on Business Central's analytics API and Microsoft's standard tools in Fabric, and run on the demo company. Not run on any customer's data.

  • Microsoft's mirroring from Business Central

    Depends on a Microsoft preview

    Not released. Microsoft's roadmap says preview in November 2026 and general availability in April 2027. When it arrives, we test it next to the standard route and switch only where it gives you something.

Models and reports

  • The model, with measures and Swedish names

    Planned

    A semantic model that reports and AI can both rely on. It isn't built yet.

  • The finance package

    Planned

    Income statement, balance sheet and a monthly overview in Power BI. Not built yet.

  • Sales, purchases and inventory

    Planned

    Packages of their own after finance, each with its own receipt where Business Central has a figure to check against.

  • Changes in plain words

    Planned

    You ask for something, such as stock turnover, see the change in Swedish, approve it and get a receipt. Nobody chats with the agents; they work in the background.

  • Ask your data with your own AI

    Planned

    With your own Copilot or Claude, under your own sign-in, against a model prepared for AI. Fabricado has no chatbot of its own. Copilot in Power BI is officially supported in English only, and we haven't tested either route in Swedish.

Keeping watch, every day

  • The overview

    Planned

    One page that says whether everything is working, or what needs you, with every step from Business Central to the report. The screens exist in the app; what moves today is a replay of demo data.

  • Data health

    Planned

    How fresh the figures are, and the check against the ledger per account and month, clear to someone who isn't technical. Per dimension comes later.

  • Monitoring and alarms

    Planned

    An email in Swedish when the night's run hasn't reported by 07:00, with what happened and what to do now. The code exists, but no alarm is sent yet: the server isn't running and no email service is connected.

  • When something goes wrong

    Planned

    Temporary errors are retried on their own, and when something would break the check, the flow stops and explains in Swedish. We won't claim that it fixes itself.

  • Updates when Microsoft changes something

    Planned

    Every new version is tested on Microsoft's demo company, built next to the old one and checked with Bryggan before the reports switch over.

What the receipt shows

Bryggan compares our copy of the general ledger with Business Central's own trial balance, per account and month. In Microsoft's demo company, all twelve months of 2025 match, on 169 accounts and 2,815 ledger lines.

On 1 November 2026 the same check ran through a pipeline in Fabric, after the year-end close, over 24 months and 4,361 ledger lines. Every difference was a proven closing entry, and SEK 0.00 was unexplained.

The comparison is done by ordinary, tested code. The next step is the comparison per dimension, and then the reports' own figures against the general ledger, once the reports exist.

How to check that a report matches

The model first, then the question

Planned

The foundation is a semantic model with measures and Swedish names. That is what makes a report and your own Copilot or Claude give the same answer.

On top of the model, each report will start with a question a finance manager actually asks, and answer it in a sentence before the chart. For example: which customers should I call this week?

The reports' figures will then be checked against the general ledger with Bryggan, after every refresh.

The packages: finance first, then sales, purchases and inventory

Where Fabricado will be built

Planned

The model, the reports and Bryggan's receipts will be built in your own Microsoft environment. Fabricado's own service should store as little as possible; we publish the list once it is tested in our sandbox.
